Tips for a clean measurement
  • Use your normal grip, mousepad and DPI. Sit like you do in game.
  • Flick to each target in one motion, then correct and click. Do not slow down to be careful: the first flick is what gets measured.
  • Windows: turn off "Enhance pointer precision". Chrome or Edge give raw input; other browsers may add OS acceleration.
  • Press Esc any time to pause. Unfinished flicks are discarded, nothing else is lost.

What the numbers mean

Every flick has a fast first movement (the primary submovement) followed by small corrections. omnisens measures where that first movement ends compared with the target. If you consistently overshoot to the right and undershoot up-left, your hand treats those directions differently, and a slightly lower sensitivity to the right and a higher one up-left evens it out. That is what the multipliers do.

Your overall sensitivity stays the same: the multipliers average to 1.0. A general overshoot in every direction is a sensitivity choice, not a directional bias, so it is reported but never applied.

Only patterns that pass a statistical test (and are consistent between two halves of your data) are applied, and only partially (the strength slider), so a profile cannot be built from noise. Your hand also adapts to any sensitivity within a few hundred flicks, so expect the benefit to be small and check it honestly with the blind A/B test.

Adapter

The omnisens adapter sits between your mouse and PC and applies the directional profile in hardware. Everything below talks to it over WebHID.

This browser cannot talk to USB devices

Configuring the adapter needs WebHID, which is available in desktop Chrome, Edge and Opera. You can still calibrate and export your values here in any browser; open this page in one of those browsers to send them to the adapter.

Connect your adapter

Plug the adapter into your PC and your mouse into the adapter, then pick omnisens in the browser's device list.

No adapter yet? The firmware runs on a Raspberry Pi Pico (RP2040) or similar board with a USB host port; see the README for parts and flashing.

Maintenance

Firmware update: after Enter bootloader a USB drive named RPI-RP2 (RP2040) or RP2350 appears. Drag the matching .uf2 file from the GitHub Releases page onto it; the adapter restarts by itself and keeps your saved profile. Factory defaults load a flat profile into RAM; Save to flash makes it stick.

About omnisens

        
Open-source directional sensitivity for any mouse. MIT licensed.

      

      

        
What it is

        
Corsair's PureVector (on the Sabre v3 Pro) calibrates your aim in Aimlabs, finds where you over- or undershoot by direction, and stores a per-direction sensitivity profile in the mouse. omnisens does the same thing for any mouse:

        
    
          
  • This page runs the calibration (a SpiderShot-style flick task), fits the profile and shows you every number.
    • 
          
  • A small USB adapter (Raspberry Pi Pico or similar, about the price of a mousepad) sits between mouse and PC and scales each movement by the gain for its direction. No drivers, no software running in the background, works in every game.
    • 
          
  • No adapter? Export the values to Raw Accel on Windows for an axis-based approximation, or just keep the numbers.
    • 
        

        
One reviewer put PureVector as like having a separate X and Y sensitivity but for more directions (DiamondLobby Reviews). The same video asks whether a profile calibrated on flicks hurts tracking. omnisens answers that with a tracking-safe symmetric mode, partial correction, a statistical evidence gate, and the blind A/B test.

      

      

        
How it differs from PureVector

        
    
          
  • Open: the firmware, the analysis and this page are on GitHub, and the profile format is documented.
    • 
          
  • Any mouse: the adapter works with any USB mouse, wired or with a USB receiver.
    • 
          
  • Transparent: you see the measured bias, the uncertainty and the exact multipliers, and you can edit them.
    • 
          
  • Honest: no profile is applied unless the data show a real pattern, and the A/B test checks whether it helps.
    • 
        

      

      

        
Limits

        
    
          
  • The adapter forwards up to 1000 Hz. Mice set to 2000-8000 Hz are reduced to 1000 Hz through it.
    • 
          
  • The calibration measures flicks. Tracking uses different motor control; if you mostly track, use tracking-safe mode and a lower strength.
    • 
          
  • Your hand adapts to any sensitivity change. Expect a few hundred flicks before a new profile settles, and recalibrate after changing grip, mousepad, DPI or posture.
    • 
          
  • Measurement noise is real. Short sessions give wide uncertainty ranges; Standard (6 rounds) or more is recommended.
    • 
        

      

      

        
Fair play

        
The adapter is a separate USB device that changes your mouse input. LAN tournaments generally forbid such devices (for example Riot's VCT global competition policy, rule 2.3.3, and the ESL rulebook, section 4.1), and online anti-cheat cannot tell it apart from other input-modifying adapters. Check the rules before using it in competition. Raw Accel is a separate driver with its own rules.
